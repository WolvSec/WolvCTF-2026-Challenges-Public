Looking at the ship, one should first notice that the shape of the hull indicates that its some sort of cargo hull (cruise ships tend to be a bit thicker and shorter). It's not crazy (though it is very weird) for a cargo ship to sit for a while if there is no demand or its defunct/junk, but such a ship usually wouldn't be in a port the entire time. Looking around, people should also notice that there are transmission towers in the water and the transmission lines seem to exclusively serve the docked ship. This should send people down a rabbit hole where they will (maybe) discover the existence of powerships which is what the ship is. There are only a handful of powerships in service in Africa. They'll find most are operated by Karpowership, which has a convenient interactive map on their website with pictures and the names of ships so people can look up their exact location on marinetracker (or other tools) and compare them to the image. Pretty quickly they should identify that the ship is the Karadeniz Powership Osman Khan in the Sekondi harbor in Ghana. The IMO is displayed on marinetracker, or can be found from a google search of the ship's name. Alternatively it is possible to geolocate the port first, then use image from google maps to ID the ship, but geolocation should be rather difficult as this isn't often considered a port and that isn't the intended solution.
